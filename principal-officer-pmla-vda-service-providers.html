What the Principal Officer role carries for VDA service providers in India

Mukesh Kumar, CAMS · Governance · November 2026

Since March 2023, businesses that provide services related to virtual digital assets (VDAs) in India have been reporting entities under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002. For anyone accountable for compliance at such a business, that change turns a technology company into something much closer to a regulated financial institution, at least for anti-money-laundering purposes.

How VDA businesses came into scope

On 7 March 2023 the Ministry of Finance issued a notification under PMLA that brought specified VDA activities within the Act. They include exchange between VDAs and fiat currencies, exchange between one or more forms of VDA, transfer of VDAs, safekeeping or administration of VDAs, and financial services related to an issuer's offer and sale of a VDA. FIU-IND followed on 10 March 2023 with AML & CFT guidelines specific to VDA service providers, which require them to register with FIU-IND as reporting entities. FIU-IND has since listed an updated version of those guidelines.

Two appointments, two different jobs

The guidelines expect a VDA service provider to appoint both a Designated Director and a separate Principal Officer. In practice I read the split this way: the Designated Director carries board-level accountability that the business meets its obligations; the Principal Officer runs the programme day to day and is the person FIU-IND deals with on reporting and compliance matters.

The Principal Officer's work therefore falls into four recurring areas:

  • Policy and procedure. KYC, customer due diligence and enhanced due diligence, sanctions and PEP screening, transaction monitoring, suspicious transaction reporting, record retention and the Travel Rule, written so that operations teams can apply them consistently.
  • Risk assessment. An enterprise-wide risk assessment covering customers, products, geographies and delivery channels, plus the crypto-specific exposures conventional models miss: unhosted wallets, mixers, darknet markets and sanctioned addresses.
  • Reporting. Reviewing and filing suspicious transaction reports and other statutory reports with FIU-IND on time and to a standard that stands up to later scrutiny.
  • Assurance and training. Independent audit of the programme, staff training, and evidence that the controls described in the policy actually operate.

Where the role is tested

The obligations are activity-based. FIU-IND's position, as reported in October 2025 when it issued notices to offshore VDA service providers, is that serving Indian users brings a provider within scope whether or not it has a physical presence in India. For a Principal Officer, that means the programme has to be defensible on its own terms, not by comparison with what other exchanges happen to do.

The test I apply is simple: could I walk the regulator from a policy clause to the control that implements it, to the alert that control produced, to the decision taken on that alert? If any link in that chain is missing, the policy is aspirational rather than operational.
