The UAE's new AML law: what changes for compliance programmes

Mukesh Kumar, CAMS · UAE · November 2026

The UAE has replaced the legal foundation of its AML/CFT regime. Federal Decree-Law No. 10 of 2025 came into effect on 14 October 2025 and repeals Federal Decree-Law No. 20 of 2018. For compliance teams serving the UAE, including virtual asset service providers in Dubai and the wider Emirates, the new law is worth reading for what it changes in practice, not only in title.

The changes I would highlight

  • Proliferation financing is in the law's own scope. The 2018 law addressed money laundering and terrorist financing; the new law adds financing of the proliferation of weapons of mass destruction.
  • Tax evasion is a predicate offence. Both direct and indirect tax evasion are captured.
  • Virtual assets are named explicitly. The law refers to the use of digital systems, virtual assets and cryptographic technologies in money laundering and terrorist financing offences.
  • A lower evidential threshold. Commentators note that the new law lowers the burden of proof for establishing a money laundering offence.
  • Corporate and management responsibility. Legal persons face significant fines where their representatives commit offences, and senior managers carry greater responsibility for preventing misconduct.
  • New oversight and powers. A higher committee now oversees the national AML/CFT committee, and authorities can suspend transactions for up to ten working days without prior notice.

What it means for a compliance programme

Three practical consequences follow. Risk assessments and policies written around the 2018 law need their references and scope updated, particularly for proliferation financing and tax-related typologies. Firms dealing in virtual assets should expect their typologies, monitoring scenarios and training to be read against a law that names virtual assets directly. And the emphasis on management responsibility makes governance evidence more important: documented decisions, escalation records and board reporting.

The new law also arrives ahead of the UAE's FATF mutual evaluation scheduled for 2026, which is reason enough to treat the update as a programme review rather than a find-and-replace exercise.
